{"j.":"Tadaksahak verbs have the structure SUBJECT PREFIX + TENSE/POLARITY MARKER + VOICE PREFIX + STEM.\n\nSubject prefixes are aɣa- ‘I’, a- ‘he/it’, and i- ‘they’. The subject prefix remains present when a noun subject is explicitly stated.\n\nTense/polarity markers are: no marker for affirmative past, b- for affirmative present/progressive, te- for affirmative future, ne- for negative past, and se- for negative present/progressive.\n\nVoice prefixes are: no prefix for active, t- for passive (‘be acted upon’), and s- for causative (‘have somebody act upon something’). The causative prefix agrees with a sibilant in the stem: it becomes z- with elmez and egzem, and š- with ukuš and išu. Thus, with iʒuwenket ‘walk’, it becomes ʒ-.\n\nSeveral verbs have different active and passive/causative stems: gon / elmez ‘swallow’, dini / ubuz ‘take’, kaw / ukuš ‘take out’, dumbu / egzem ‘slaughter’, and nin / išu ‘drink’. Other stems shown are egmi ‘look for’, uswud ‘watch’, and efred ‘gather’.\n\nPhrase order is subject–verb–object. An independent subject can be omitted because the verb identifies it. Object pronouns follow the verb: a means ‘him/it’, and i means ‘them’. In passive phrases, the affected entity is the subject, preceding the verb. Causative objects likewise follow the verb; the person performing the caused action need not be expressed.\n\nThese rules give the following answers to 5.1:\na. ‘The water was not drunk’: a-ne-t-išu.\nb. ‘I had the sheep watched’: aɣa-s-uswud.\nc. ‘The salt will be swallowed’: a-te-t-elmez.\nd. ‘He is not taking the corpse’: a-se-dini.\n\nFor 5.2:\ne. absubuz aryen — a-b-s-ubuz: ‘He is having the water taken.’\nf. aɣabʒiʒuwenket i — aɣa-b-ʒ-iʒuwenket: ‘I’m having them walked.’\ng. amanokal anenin aryen — a-ne-nin: ‘The chief did not drink the water.’\nh. cidi anetegmi — a-ne-t-egmi: ‘The salt was not looked for.’\ni. atesefred cidi — a-te-s-efred: ‘He will have the salt gathered.’"}
