{"a.":"The first word is the subject. The second contains the object’s root followed by the action: -xi ‘waited for’, -mų ‘saw’, or -mąt'ę ‘hit’.\n\nThe noun roots are pę ‘deer’, ka ‘mother’, hų ‘cedar’, ki ‘blanket’, p'ǫy ‘brother’, ci ‘eye’, t'oy ‘person’, p'įw ‘sparrow’, and p'i ‘head’.\n\nThere are two noun classes:\n• Deer, mother, brother, person and sparrow take -na in the singular and -nemą in the plural.\n• Cedar, blanket, eye and head take -ne in the plural and -nemą in the singular.\nThus -nemą reverses a noun’s usual number.\n\nPossessive prefixes are ą- ‘his’, ką- ‘your’, ki- ‘our’, and i- ‘their’. Between the possessive prefix and noun root, ordinary singular forms have no additional marker, ordinary plurals have w-, and reversed-number forms have m-.\n\nThe verb prefix depends on the subject’s actual number and the object’s number form. The combinations demonstrated are:\n• Singular subject + -na object: no prefix.\n• Singular subject + reversed-number object: i-.\n• Plural subject + -na object: i-.\n• Plural subject + -ne object: iw-.\n• Plural subject + reversed-number object: ipi-.\n\nFor example, kimpęnemą means ‘our deers’: ki- marks possession and m-…-nemą marks reversed number, hence plural for ‘deer’. In ipihųmų, ipi- marks a plural subject with a reversed-number object; ‘cedar’ is normally plural, so the object is singular. Finally, -mų means ‘saw’."}
