In passive-like clauses, the patient can precede the verb (cidi atetegmi, “the salt will be looked for”). Pronouns can also occur after the verb, as in atedini a, “he will take it.” The verb forms mark features such as person, time/aspect, negation, and whether an action is caused or undergone. For example, aɣa- marks “I” in the examples; a- occurs with “he,” and i- with “they.” The examples contrast future forms such as ate-dini with past forms such as aɣa-gon. Forms with s, as in asegmi (“had them looked for”), express having an action done; forms such as anetubuz and asetefred are negative. The data do not establish every boundary or rule uniquely, so these are tentative generalizations.
