{"(i)":"The spelling omits predictable [ə] vowels and [ʔ] consonants. The main rules are:\n\n• Syllabify from right to left. A syllable has an onset, a vowel and at most one coda consonant. Consonant + r clusters can be onsets before written vowels, as in trutru, but not before inserted [ə]. Insert [ə] where necessary to syllabify the remaining consonants. Immediately before an onset b, d, g or nz, only a nasal can serve as the preceding coda; otherwise another [ə] is needed. Thus brbr becomes [ᵐbə.ɾə.ᵐbəɾ], whereas bthfrnm becomes [ᵐbəθ.ɸəɾ.nəm].\n\n• b, d, g and nz represent prenasalized consonants. In onsets they are [ᵐb, ⁿd, ᵑg, ⁿdz]; in codas they are devoiced to [ᵐp, ⁿt, ᵑk, ⁿts]. Similarly, th is [ð] in an onset but [θ] in a coda. These digraphs each represent one consonant.\n\n• z normally represents [ts]. Directly before the front vowels ä, e, i and ü, z and nz become [tʃ] and [ⁿdʒ]. An intervening r prevents this change: compare nzigfu with nzrugrm.\n\n• ä represents [æ], ü represents [y], and o is [ɔ] in an open syllable but [ɞ] in a closed syllable. The contrast between emoth and emothdbo demonstrates the last rule.\n\n• f represents [ɸ], r represents [ɾ], and y represents [j]. The other ordinary letters retain their displayed sound values.\n\n• Insert [ʔ] before a word-initial vowel and between adjacent vowels, as in ezi and brüziä. Mark the resulting syllable boundaries with full stops."}
