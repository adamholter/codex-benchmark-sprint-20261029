  "(i)": "The spelling omits predictable schwas and uses the same letters for sounds whose pronunciation depends on their position.\n\nVowels: a, ä, e, i, u and ü represent [a, æ, e, i, u, y]. The letter o is [ɔ] in open syllables but [ɞ] in closed syllables: compare emoth [ʔe.mɞθ] with emothdbo [ʔe.mɔ.ðə.ⁿdə.ᵐbɔ]. Insert [ʔ] before a word-initial vowel and between adjacent vowels.\n\nConsonants: f = [ɸ], r = [ɾ], and y = [j]. The digraphs th and nz each represent one consonant; ng is instead n followed by g. In syllable onsets, b, d, g and nz are [ᵐb, ⁿd, ᵑg, ⁿdz]; in codas they become [ᵐp, ⁿt, ᵑk, ⁿts]. Similarly, th is [ð] in onsets and [θ] in codas. Before front vowels (e, i, ä, ü), z and onset nz are palatalised to [tʃ] and [ⁿdʒ]; otherwise they are [ts] and [ⁿdz].\n\nSyllabification: work from right to left, inserting unwritten [ə] where consonants cannot be accommodated around written vowels. A syllable can have one coda consonant. Onsets can contain a consonant followed by r before a written vowel; such clusters are not formed before an inserted schwa. Before an onset b, d, g or nz, only a nasal can serve as the preceding coda; otherwise another schwa is needed. These patterns explain, for example, brbr [ᵐbə.ɾə.ᵐbəɾ] versus brüziä [ᵐbɾy.tʃi.ʔæ].\n\nTo recover the spelling from a transcription, reverse these rules: omit predictable [ə] and [ʔ], and replace positional sound variants with their common spelling."
