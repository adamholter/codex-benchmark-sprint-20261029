{"j.":"Tadaksahak phrases can have the order subject–verb–object: for example, feji abnin aryen means ‘The sheep is drinking the water.’ A pronominal object follows the verb, as in atedini a ‘He will take it.’ With a passive verb, the affected noun is the subject and precedes the verb: hamu anetubuz means ‘The meat was not taken.’ A third-person subject can also be expressed by the verb without a separate noun.\n\nThe verb forms contain a stem together with material indicating the subject, tense or polarity, and whether the action is passive or causative. The examples show:\n• aɣa- marks a first-person singular subject: aɣagon ‘I swallowed’ and aɣakaw ‘I took out.’\n• i- occurs in affirmative past forms with third-person subjects, as in idumbu ‘they slaughtered.’ Third-person a- occurs in several other forms.\n• -te- marks the future in atedini ‘he will take it.’ Another -te- marks passive voice: compare cidi atetegmi ‘the salt will be looked for,’ where both occur, with itegzem ‘they were slaughtered.’\n• -ne- marks negation in past forms, as in anetubuz ‘was not taken’; -se- marks negation in present forms, as in asetefred ‘is not being gathered.’\n• Causative forms express having somebody perform an action. They contain a consonant such as s, z or š before the stem, as in asegmi ‘he had them looked for,’ atezelmez ‘he will have [it] swallowed,’ and anešišu ‘he did not have [it] drunk.’ The causative material and adjacent vowels vary with the verb form.\n\nThus aryen anetišu means ‘The water was not drunk’; aɣasuswud feji means ‘I am having the sheep watched’; cidi atetelmez means ‘The salt will be swallowed’; and asedini jifa means ‘He had the corpse taken.’ For the other requested constructions, the same patterns combine a causative with ‘take,’ ‘walk,’ or ‘gather,’ or a past negative with ‘drink’ or passive ‘look for.’ The supplied examples establish these combinations of meanings, though the exact form of every new combination is not independently illustrated."}
