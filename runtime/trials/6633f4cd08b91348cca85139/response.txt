{"(i)":"The orthography generally writes consonants and the vowels other than [ə]. An unwritten [ə] is supplied where needed to make syllables, as in frf [ɸə.ɾəɸ]. Syllable division can be inferred from the examples: a consonant before a vowel generally begins a syllable, while a consonant following a vowel may close it. Some clusters, such as [tɾ] in trutru, can begin a syllable.\n\nA written vowel at the beginning of a word is preceded by [ʔ], as in ezi [ʔe.tʃi]. Adjacent written vowels are separated by [ʔ], as in trnää [təɾ.næ.ʔæ]. The letter o is pronounced [ɔ] in an open syllable and [ɞ] in a closed syllable, as illustrated by emothdbo and emoth.\n\nSeveral consonants change according to their position. At the beginning of a syllable, b, d and g are [ᵐb], [ⁿd] and [ᵑg]; at the end, they are [ᵐp], [ⁿt] and [ᵑk]. Likewise, th is [ð] at the beginning of a syllable and [θ] at the end. The letter z is [tʃ] before a front vowel, but [ts] elsewhere; nz is correspondingly [ⁿdʒ] before a front vowel and [ⁿdz] at the beginning of a syllable elsewhere, but [ⁿts] at the end of a syllable. Other correspondences include f [ɸ] and r [ɾ]."}
